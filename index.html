Force for Free:
Target-Error-Augmented State Representation for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Abstract

Contact-rich manipulation remains challenging for visuomotor policies because vision captures geometry well but provides limited information about interaction forces. Existing approaches bridge this gap with dedicated force/tactile sensors or complex force estimators, increasing cost and complexity. We introduce Target-Error-Augmented State (TEAS), a simple drop-in state representation that turns impedance-control tracking residuals into implicit force cues for end-to-end policy learning. The key idea is that, under impedance control, the discrepancy between the commanded target and the actual robot state is not merely a control residual; it is a structured signature of contact. TEAS exploits this by replacing the conventional proprioceptive input with the concatenation of the current state, the commanded target, and their tracking error, without modifying the network architecture or training a separate force model. On a low-cost real robot equipped only with RGB cameras and no force, torque, or tactile sensors, TEAS consistently transforms performance across five contact-rich tasks, achieving approximately 73.9% higher success rates. It remains robust to spatial variations and generalizes to alternative control and teleoperation settings on representative tasks. These results show that control residuals can serve as a practical and scalable alternative to explicit force instrumentation for contact-rich manipulation.
